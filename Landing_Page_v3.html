Schemata
Former Marine-Founder 5x Revenue Growth Stanford Founder Investing Again (2X)
Reservation Deadline
Thu July 23 · 12:00pm ET
Full Deal Room →

Elevator Pitch: Schemata builds proprietary spatial AI that turns ordinary video and technical manuals into interactive 3D training — an AI instructor that guides users through operating, maintaining, and repairing military equipment. It has captured ~$5.2M in contracts across the Air Force, Navy, and Army (with $100M and $99M contract ceilings in reach), grown revenue ~6x to $1.7M in FY25, and is raising a $15M Series A at a $75M post-money valuation co-led by Shield Capital and Wellington Management. AV first invested at the seed and is doubling down.

Founders James Brown, CEO — former Marine Corps Infantry & Information Operations officer (top 1% of officers) who oversaw a ~$2B science-and-technology program and briefed the Secretary of the Navy; Stanford MBA and MS in Symbolic Systems, where the platform's research began. Huy Nguyen, CTO — Stanford Virtual Human Interaction Lab researcher and Gates Scholar (MS Computer Science). COO Quin Roberts adds federal policy experience from the U.S. House; Head of Federal Sales Miles Silverstein is a former Green Beret with an MIT Sloan MBA. Former DIU Director Michael Brown (ex-Symantec CEO) joins the board.
AV's Access
AV invested in Schemata's seed round in December 2024 and is being offered Series A allocation on the strength of that relationship. The $15M round is co-led by Shield Capital — a leading defense-tech VC (Armada, Code Metal, HawkEye 360) whose co-founders ran the Defense Innovation Unit — and Wellington Management ($1.1T AUM; ~$8B private platform behind Scale AI and Affirm), each committing $5M, or two-thirds of the round. It drew four competing term sheets and is ~2x oversubscribed, with Shield partner and former DIU Director Michael Brown taking the board seat.
Why Invest in Schemata
  • A team built for both technology and procurement: Technical founders developed the underlying spatial-AI platform at Stanford, while military and federal operators understand the end user, the procurement process, and the relationships that turn pilots into programs — a combination that has already produced contracts across three service branches.
  • Strong, repeatable tri-service traction: Paying work spans the Air Force, Navy, and Army; the Air Force deployment grew organically to nine installations and 130+ users, and multi-year contract vehicles ($100M Air Force ceiling, $99M Army OTA) let Schemata expand without recompeting for each new order.
  • A large defense beachhead with improving economics: The DoW spends ~$14B a year on virtual training; Schemata runs ~92% unit gross margins, with the marginal cost of a new asset modeled to fall from ~$3K toward ~$300 as automation compounds — inside a broader opportunity the company estimates near $400B.
Key Issues
  • Revenue conversion and budget timing: Headline contract ceilings are not yet committed revenue; continuing resolutions, budget delays, or shifting priorities could defer it even where demand is strong.
  • Security and accreditation could gate adoption: Scaling into sensitive programs depends on Impact Level 4/5 accreditation; a May 2026 cybersecurity incident in a separate prototype environment (no controlled information exposed, per management) is still under forensic review.
  • Entry price versus stage: $75M post-money is ~12.3x projected FY26 revenue, so execution against the $6M FY26 and $15M+ FY27 plan is needed to justify the entry.

Spiko
Top 20 VC Lead 5x Revenue Growth Ex-French Treasury Ex-Palantir
Reservation Deadline
Thu July 23 · 12:00pm ET
Full Deal Room →

Elevator Pitch: Spiko lets European businesses earn a daily yield on idle cash by holding it in low-risk government debt — with same-day withdrawals, no lock-up, built on its own regulatory license and tokenized settlement rails. Since the end of 2024 it has grown assets under management from €130M to €1.5B (~$1.7B) — 11x in 16 months — across 3,500 business users in 25+ countries, making it the largest platform of its kind in Europe. NEA is pre-empting and leading its Series B at a €366M pre-money valuation, and the round is 3x oversubscribed.

Founders Paul-Adrien Hyppolite, Co-Founder & CEO — former Deputy Head of the Savings & Financial Markets Division at the French Treasury, where he regulated the exact eurozone market Spiko now operates in; degrees from École normale supérieure and École polytechnique. Antoine Michon, Co-Founder & COO — a former deployment lead at Palantir and ministerial adviser on the French state's digital transformation; CTO Samuel Briole brings prior startup engineering experience. Series A and angel backers include the founder & CEO of Revolut (~$115B), the CTO of Wise (~$11B), Blackstone co-CIO Lionel Assant, and Kyriba founder Jean-Luc Robert.
AV's Access
AV's access runs through its relationship with NEA and its recent European expansion, including the launch of its London office — positioning AV as a credible partner in a European-led round rather than an outside participant. NEA (Philip Chopin) pre-empted the round with a €26M lead commitment, and Index Ventures, the Series A lead, is returning at roughly 2x its pro-rata (~€15M). The round is 3x oversubscribed. AV's allocation spans both open tranches — B1 at the original €366M pre-money price and a step-up B2 tranche.
Why Invest in Spiko
  • A high-conviction, highly relevant syndicate: NEA — one of the world's oldest and largest VC firms (Robinhood, Uber, Plaid) — pre-empted the round after building a thesis on tokenized financial infrastructure, and Index Ventures is investing ~2x its pro-rata. The round is 3x oversubscribed, and prior backers include the operators behind Europe's two most valuable fintechs: the CEO of Revolut and the CTO of Wise.
  • Infrastructure economics behind a regulatory moat: Spiko issues and settles its own fund on tokenized rails it built and owns — keeping the full fee instead of renting a custodian's back office — and holds the AMF and MiFID licenses to run a fund across the EU. Each new euro of AUM costs almost nothing to service, so a ~12bps fee already throws off a ~$2.2M run rate on ~30 people, while US-only players like Ondo, Securitize, and Superstate can't legally serve a business in Paris or Frankfurt.
  • The settlement layer for AI agents: Spiko's near-term story is yield on idle cash, but its 24/7, programmable settlement is the money-movement layer autonomous AI agents need to transact without a human in the loop. NEA puts a small but non-trivial probability on Spiko becoming the most consequential fintech infrastructure company built this decade.
Key Issues
  • Rate sensitivity: Adoption tracks the yield gap, which tracks the ECB's policy rate; if rates fall back toward zero, the reason many customers first sign up narrows. Fees are charged on AUM (not the yield), and Europe's money-market industry held roughly flat through the 2014–2022 zero-rate era — but this is the single biggest variable in the growth rate.
  • Geographic and customer concentration: About 90% of AUM sits in France, and the top 100 accounts hold ~62% of assets (the largest ~6.6%, near €100M). Treasury cash is liquid by design, so a few large accounts leaving — or French growth stalling before the Western-Europe push lands — would pull the fee run-rate down fast.
  • Thin fee, long path to scale: The ~12bps management fee (~$2.2M run rate) doesn't yet cover ~€5M of annual cost; fees alone clear that base only around the $5B AUM target (~3x today). Protocol-partnership revenue (~$13M in 2026) covers the gap and makes Spiko cash-flow positive now, but those blockchain-foundation incentives may not recur at that scale.

A Note on Portfolio Strategy

Smart venture portfolios are built deliberately over years, with enough scale and diversification to give the asset class room to work.

Building a venture allocation solely through single-deal investments often requires significantly more capital and time, vs. a planned mix of funds and syndications.

Many investors have a Venture Plan with AV (see the "Planning" tab above), and dedicate approximately 50%–75% of their annual venture allocation to one or more AV funds, then supplement with selected syndication offerings. Submitted plans are not legally binding. Investors who submit plans unlock more personalized service.
