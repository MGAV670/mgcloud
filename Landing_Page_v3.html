Volantis
Sam Altman Invested Serial Founder ✦ Thiel Fellow ✦ Patent Holder
Reservation Deadline
Thu July 23 · 12:00pm ET
Full Deal Room →

Elevator Pitch: Backed by Sam Altman, Jeff Dean, and $96M in signed purchase orders, Volantis is rearchitecting AI inference — swapping copper's electrical lanes for light-based "photonic wires" so a single accelerator can be surrounded by ~10TB of memory (roughly 35x an NVIDIA GPU) and run frontier, trillion-parameter models 30x faster, 15x cheaper, and 6x more efficiently than today's GPUs, at an $81.4M Series A led by Abstract Ventures ($250M pre-money).

Founder Tapa Ghosh, CEO — Thiel Fellow and serial chip entrepreneur who forwent college in 2015 to found Vathys (YC W18), a 3D AI semiconductor company backed by deep-tech firm DCVC; he has taken three chips to silicon tape-out and holds four patents. He is joined by CTO Roy Meade (led Micron's High-Bandwidth Memory group and was Ayar Labs' first hire; 120+ patents), VP of Packaging Beth Keser (ex-Head of Systems & Packaging at Intel), and Chief Architect Partha Kundu (ex-Broadcom, ex-Microsoft AI, ex-Oracle). The team has collectively brought 15 chip designs to tape-out.
AV's Access
AV secured an allocation in the $81.4M Series A co-led by Abstract Ventures ($1.8B AUM; early backer of xAI, SpaceX, Crusoe, and Replit, taking $15M and a board seat) and Old Tree, the firm of former Stripe executive Lachy Groom ($15M). Co-investors include VXI ($25M), Triatomic Capital, and Magnetar. The company's angel cap table reads like a who's-who of AI — Sam Altman (OpenAI), Jeff Dean (Google), Alex Wang (Meta), Naval Ravikant (AngelList), and Dylan Patel (SemiAnalysis).
Why Invest in Volantis
  • A massive value proposition: Volantis attacks the two bottlenecks throttling frontier AI — memory capacity and memory bandwidth — that have historically been a forced trade-off. Its photonic wires reach ~100x farther at ~30x the bandwidth of copper, letting one system run trillion-to-quadrillion-parameter models that conventional GPU clusters must shard across 40+ chips.
  • Rare pre-silicon commercial traction: The company has booked $96M in purchase orders and upfront payments — including $70M from a top-five neocloud — more than a year before its first shipment. That scale of committed demand is highly unusual for a semiconductor company at this stage.
  • An exceptional, capital-efficient team: Leaders held senior roles at Broadcom, Intel, Micron, and Ayar Labs, and the cap table adds the CEO of OpenAI, the Chief Scientist of Google, and the Chief AI Officer of Meta. Volantis proved key building blocks on only ~$17M raised prior — a fraction of peers who spent $700M–$1.8B to reach first silicon.
Key Issues
  • System integration risk: The core photonic interposer is proven, but Volantis still must integrate optics, HBM, logic, packaging, thermals, and software into a reliable data-center accelerator — the composition is where many hardware programs slip.
  • Performance translation: Headline figures are simulated; the company must prove they hold on real customer workloads once full-system silicon returns in 2027, with first shipment not expected until H2 2027.
  • Adoption and concentration: Even strong hardware must overcome NVIDIA's entrenched software ecosystem and procurement inertia, and today's bookings rest on a small number of relationships.

Schemata
Former Marine-Founder 5x Revenue Growth Stanford Founder Investing Again (2X)

Thu July 23 · 12:00pm ET

Elevator Pitch: Schemata builds proprietary spatial AI that turns ordinary video and technical manuals into interactive 3D training — an AI instructor that guides users through operating, maintaining, and repairing military equipment. It has captured ~$5.2M in contracts across the Air Force, Navy, and Army (with $100M and $99M contract ceilings in reach), grown revenue ~6x to $1.7M in FY25, and is raising a $15M Series A at a $75M post-money valuation co-led by Shield Capital and Wellington Management. AV first invested at the seed and is doubling down.

Founders James Brown, CEO — former Marine Corps Infantry & Information Operations officer (top 1% of officers) who oversaw a ~$2B science-and-technology program and briefed the Secretary of the Navy; Stanford MBA and MS in Symbolic Systems, where the platform's research began. Huy Nguyen, CTO — Stanford Virtual Human Interaction Lab researcher and Gates Scholar (MS Computer Science). COO Quin Roberts adds federal policy experience from the U.S. House; Head of Federal Sales Miles Silverstein is a former Green Beret with an MIT Sloan MBA. Former DIU Director Michael Brown (ex-Symantec CEO) joins the board.
AV's Access
AV invested in Schemata's seed round in December 2024 and is being offered Series A allocation on the strength of that relationship. The $15M round is co-led by Shield Capital — a leading defense-tech VC (Armada, Code Metal, HawkEye 360) whose co-founders ran the Defense Innovation Unit — and Wellington Management ($1.1T AUM; ~$8B private platform behind Scale AI and Affirm), each committing $5M, or two-thirds of the round. It drew four competing term sheets and is ~2x oversubscribed, with Shield partner and former DIU Director Michael Brown taking the board seat.
Why Invest in Schemata
  • A team built for both technology and procurement: Technical founders developed the underlying spatial-AI platform at Stanford, while military and federal operators understand the end user, the procurement process, and the relationships that turn pilots into programs — a combination that has already produced contracts across three service branches.
  • Strong, repeatable tri-service traction: Paying work spans the Air Force, Navy, and Army; the Air Force deployment grew organically to nine installations and 130+ users, and multi-year contract vehicles ($100M Air Force ceiling, $99M Army OTA) let Schemata expand without recompeting for each new order.
  • A large defense beachhead with improving economics: The DoW spends ~$14B a year on virtual training; Schemata runs ~92% unit gross margins, with the marginal cost of a new asset modeled to fall from ~$3K toward ~$300 as automation compounds — inside a broader opportunity the company estimates near $400B.
Key Issues
  • Revenue conversion and budget timing: Headline contract ceilings are not yet committed revenue; continuing resolutions, budget delays, or shifting priorities could defer it even where demand is strong.
  • Security and accreditation could gate adoption: Scaling into sensitive programs depends on Impact Level 4/5 accreditation; a May 2026 cybersecurity incident in a separate prototype environment (no controlled information exposed, per management) is still under forensic review.
  • Entry price versus stage: $75M post-money is ~12.3x projected FY26 revenue, so execution against the $6M FY26 and $15M+ FY27 plan is needed to justify the entry.

A Note on Portfolio Strategy

Smart venture portfolios are built deliberately over years, with enough scale and diversification to give the asset class room to work.

Building a venture allocation solely through single-deal investments often requires significantly more capital and time, vs. a planned mix of funds and syndications.

Many investors have a Venture Plan with AV (see the "Planning" tab above), and dedicate approximately 50%–75% of their annual venture allocation to one or more AV funds, then supplement with selected syndication offerings.
