Riven Systems
Pre-Seed Critical Minerals Initial Berkeley Founder
Reservation Deadline
Thu July 9 · 12:00pm ET
Full Deal Room →

Elevator Pitch: China controls ~90% of global rare earth separation capacity — not because the ore isn't here, but because no one has been able to crack the processing chokepoint outside of Beijing. Riven Systems is an AI-driven "Separation Intelligence" platform that collapses a years-long, warehouse-scale chemical development process into weeks, and deploys modular separation nodes directly at customer sites — targeting ~60% CapEx reduction and ~75% OpEx reduction against the incumbent model, with first commercial revenue targeted Q1 2027.

Founders Kurt "Chip" Breitenkamp, CEO — PhD Polymer Science (UMass); VP of Materials Research at Radical AI (built its self-driving lab for alloy discovery); President of NanoGraf Corporation (raised $150M+, scaled lab chemistry to commercial 18650 battery cell production); co-inventor on 22+ international patent families. Orion Archer Cohen, CTO — PhD Chemistry (UC Berkeley); led computational materials team at Radical AI; authored TorchSim (widely used open-source molecular dynamics framework); ARPA-E alumnus; active collaborator with NVIDIA's ALCHEMI materials-AI team.
AV's Access
Blue Ivy Ventures led diligence and secured AV's allocation. Cerberus Capital Management — a $65B alternative-asset manager with a documented critical-minerals portfolio including a rare earth stake in Torngat Metals (the largest rare earth deposit in North America) — is leading the round with a $3M commitment and a board seat. New System Ventures, the company's incubating investor since November 2025, is returning with ~$500K alongside AV.
Why Invest in Riven Systems
  • The founders have already built and scaled the exact playbook Riven is running: Chip built a self-driving lab for AI-guided materials discovery at Radical AI and took NanoGraf from lab chemistry to commercial battery cell production with $150M+ raised. Orion built the AI-driven alloy discovery platform at Radical AI and authored TorchSim. They are not adapting experience from adjacent fields — they are rerunning a proven operational model in a new market.
  • The macro tailwind is real and accelerating: China's rare earth export controls have escalated in three successive rounds since April 2025, and U.S. federal critical-mineral investment has exceeded $15B since 2025. This is one of the few deep-tech bets where government policy is actively expanding the customer's willingness to pay in real time — Riven does not have to create demand, it has to capture it.
  • Nine months old, five enterprise counterparties already at "agreed terms" stage: Active LOI-stage conversations with MP Materials, USA Rare Earth, and Sims Limited upstream, and Lockheed Martin, Bloom Energy, and Ford downstream. Two-sided traction from the most credible names in the critical-minerals and defense supply chain — achieved before the self-driving lab was even commissioned — is a hard signal to generate at any stage, let alone pre-seed.
Key Issues
  • IP moat is a near-term plan, not a current asset: One provisional patent filed (Feb 2026, covering platform architecture); the three composition-of-matter patents on Riven's novel ligands — the IP that would most directly block a fast follower — are all still in drafting status with target filing dates through Q4 2027.
  • Cerberus' mining portfolio could create channel-conflict risk: The same critical-minerals relationships that make Cerberus useful as a lead could make independent miners (MP Materials, USA Rare Earth) hesitant to hand a Cerberus-backed vendor their process data. Riven's Separation-as-a-Service model depends on being seen as a neutral, multi-customer platform.
  • Pre-revenue, deep-tech hardware timelines carry execution risk: First commercial revenue is targeted Q1 2027. The path from self-driving lab throughput to signed contracts to deployed separation nodes involves materials science milestones, customer procurement cycles, and hardware manufacturing that all run on longer timelines than software.

Elevator Pitch: The four executives who scaled Proofpoint from $70M to a $12.5B acquisition have built an AI avatar platform that cuts enterprise sales rep ramp time by 50–67%, is delivering 179% net revenue retention, and is already trusted by Paychex, Netskope, and Masimo — backed by Lightspeed's Arif Janmohamed, who liked this team so much he left Lightspeed after 18 years and bet his new firm on them.

Founders David Knight, CEO — EVP & GM at Proofpoint, built three products from $0 to $500M, previously VP of Product at WebEx (acquired by Cisco for $3.3B); co-founders Matt Huang (COO/Product, Proofpoint & McKinsey), Scot McIntosh (Engineering, Proofpoint & 14 years at Microsoft), and Christopher Iezzoni (AI, Proofpoint) all scaled together through Proofpoint's $12.5B exit.
AV's Access
Chestnut Street Ventures led diligence and secured AV's co-investor allocation alongside Lightspeed Venture Partners, which led the $8M Seed and is now committing $13.45M to the Series A lead. Arif Janmohamed (Forbes Midas List 2026) has since left Lightspeed to found Duration Ventures and chose Avarra as one of Duration's first investments.
Why Invest in Avarra
  • A proven team with a directly comparable exit: The four co-founders scaled Proofpoint together from $70M to $1B+ through a $12.5B acquisition. David Knight alone built three $0-to-$500M products inside one company. This is not a first-time team learning on the job — it is the same group, running the same enterprise playbook, with an AI-native product in a market they know cold.
  • 179% net revenue retention signals a system of record, not a point tool: When Paychex — already an active Gong customer — evaluated Gong's own roleplay module, called it "low-fidelity," and expanded Avarra to 1,063 seats across six divisions instead, that is product-market fit. Customers embed their top performers' expertise into the platform; displacing Avarra means dismantling the institutional knowledge infrastructure built on top of it.
  • The lead investor's conviction is personal: Arif Janmohamed led Avarra's Seed at Lightspeed, then departed to co-found Duration Ventures and made Avarra one of his first investments there. He draws a direct line from this bet to his Series A investments in Netskope, Navan, and Moveworks, which collectively returned billions. His framework is explicit: he underwrites the founder, not just the market.
Key Issues
  • Revenue concentration: Paychex (~$980K ACV) and Masimo (~$500K ACV) together represent ~44% of contracted revenue. Masimo is being acquired by Danaher in a $9.9B deal — acquirers routinely consolidate vendors post-close, creating near-term uncertainty even as Masimo actively expands its Avarra footprint.
  • Founder-led to structured sales motion: The deals that closed Paychex, Netskope, and Masimo ran through David Knight directly. The Series A plan triples headcount in 18 months and requires hiring a senior CRO — a transition from a founder-led motion that has not yet been stress-tested.
  • Gross margins not yet fully locked in: Current ~68% gross margins are partly supported by compute credits and reserved-instance pricing not yet fully contracted. The company's first VP of Finance joined the week of June 22 to begin formalizing the cost structure.

Elevator Pitch: MagicX is becoming the intent layer every AI product runs on — a usage-based SDK that predicts what users mean before they finish typing, already pulling 500+ inbound enterprise requests from Nubank, Google, Adobe, and Atlassian with zero sales effort and two signed customers within the first month of outreach.

Founders Brad Kowalk, CEO — ~5 years at Meta leading product across the AI New Experiences team, applying LLMs across one of the internet's largest user bases; Seung Woo Lee, CTO — EM at Meta on Facebook Stories (across all four apps simultaneously); founding member of Uber Maps (through Microsoft acquisition). Angels: Arash Ferdowsi (Dropbox co-founder) and Ime Archibong (former Facebook VP).
AV's Access
Green D Ventures & Purple Arch Ventures led diligence and secured AV's allocation. Alumni Ventures participated in MagicX's June 2025 Seed SAFE — making this a follow-on investment — and AV's prior conviction earned us a seat at the table alongside Forerunner Ventures (Eurie Kim, Managing Partner), which is returning with a $7.5M commitment. Advance Venture Partners (AVP) is co-leading with a $9M check and a board seat.
Why Invest in MagicX
  • Infrastructure, not an app — a defensible toll road on every natural language interface: AI Autocomplete is priced at $0.004 per parameter (pure usage-based), creating compounding revenue as every deployment trains the action index. Comparable infrastructure plays — Algolia (~$2.3B), Bloomreach (~$2.2B) — validate the category. No competing product exists today.
  • Unprecedented inbound demand, near-zero CAC: 500+ enterprise inbound requests from a single post, two signed enterprise customers (Nubank at 100M users, ActiveCampaign at $290M ARR) closed within the first month of outreach — entirely inbound. The go-to-market is a self-serve SDK with a three-week proof of value that auto-converts to subscription.
  • Four-layer moat with a granted patent at its core: Protection compounds across (1) a granted patent on real-time parameter prediction, (2) infrastructure complexity spanning five integrated technologies, (3) the action index data flywheel that improves with cross-customer learning, and (4) switching cost — removing the SDK returns users to a blank text box.
Key Issues
  • Pipeline is promise, not revenue: Only two live customers at close; the $90M valuation is priced on what's coming. Enterprise procurement cycles could delay conversion of the 500+ inbound waitlist.
  • Self-serve SDK launch is untested at scale: The shift from 8-week custom builds to self-serve (launching mid-July 2026) is unproven. Current engineering capacity of 8 is insufficient to serve the full pipeline without the Series A hiring plan executing quickly.
  • Platform players carry the same roadmap: OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic all have direct incentive to solve cold-start intent natively within their own ecosystems, which could push MagicX toward the long tail if incumbents move first.

A Note on Portfolio Strategy

Smart venture portfolios are built deliberately over years, with enough scale and diversification to give the asset class room to work.

Building a venture allocation solely through single-deal investments often requires significantly more capital and time, vs. a planned mix of funds and syndications.

Many investors have a Venture Plan with AV (see the "Planning" tab above), and dedicate approximately 50%–75% of their annual venture allocation to one or more AV funds, then supplement with selected syndication offerings. Submitted plans are not legally binding. Investors who submit plans unlock more personalized service.
