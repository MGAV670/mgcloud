Tera AI
Top 10 Investor, CB Insights Ex-Google PhD Founder 3x Revenue Growth
Reservation Deadline
Thu July 16 · 12:00pm ET
Full Deal Room →

Elevator Pitch: A Caltech-trained team out of Google X and DeepMind has built a military-grade AI "world model" that lets any vehicle navigate using only an ordinary camera when GPS is jammed — already earning real revenue on a Firestorm production contract, down-selected by the DoD, and building with Anduril, at an $80M pre-money entry.

Founder Tony Zhang, CEO — Caltech AI PhD advised by Pietro Perona (widely called the "godfather of vision AI"); Machine Learning Lead at Google X building geospatial foundation models; worked with DeepMind on the model later released as AlphaEarth. The 11-person team includes an inventor of the diffusion model behind modern image and video generation, with a bench drawn from DeepMind, MIT, Caltech, and Shield AI. Advisors include Perona, Caltech's Yisong Yue, and GPS pioneer Charlie Trimble.
AV's Access
Ring Ventures led diligence and secured AV's allocation alongside lead investor Tamarack Global — a defense and deep-tech specialist ($200M+ AUM) anchoring the round with up to $15M, led by Founder & Managing Partner John McCormick (board member at Figure AI and Fuse Energy). Existing backers Felicis and Inovia are expected to return, with strategic interest from In-Q-Tel (the CIA's venture arm) and Japanese and Korean OEMs. AV has worked alongside Tamarack before, including Venus Aerospace and Impulse Space.
Why Invest in Tera AI
  • An exceptional technical team solving a deceptively hard problem: GPS-denied navigation from a camera feed alone demands mastery of computer vision, real-time 3D reconstruction, and edge optimization. Tera's founder trained under the architect of modern vision AI and built geospatial models at DeepMind; the team includes a diffusion-model inventor and researchers from DeepMind, MIT, Caltech, and Shield AI.
  • Government pull and proven demand: Firestorm is on a multi-year production contract licensing Tera's model at $500/unit — real revenue with real delivery schedules — and the DoD has down-selected Tera (one of ~50) for an Air Force effort while it builds with Anduril. The DoD is moving to embed Tera-like capability directly into procurement requirements.
  • A defensible data and architecture moat: Exclusive data partnerships with top OEMs across air, ground, and on-road create a unique multi-domain training corpus that competitors cannot easily assemble, reinforced by two filed patents. The product is software-only and passive — a camera emits no signal to target — which is exactly what the DoD is prioritizing.
Key Issues
  • Bookings are early and concentrated: ~$6M ARR and a path to ~$12M by year-end rest on a small number of relationships plus federal awards that are notoriously slow and non-linear. Only two customers are in production today; the growth story depends on converting the pipeline.
  • A well-funded competitive field: Defense autonomy is hot — Scout AI recently raised a $100M Series A, the neo-primes are investing heavily, and a software-only product can attract fast-followers. Tera's moats are real but will need sustained execution to harden.
  • Federal GTM build-out is unproven: The plan hinges on standing up a Washington federal go-to-market team and roughly tripling engineering (15 → 40) over 18 months, alongside ITAR/CMMC readiness that is still in progress.

Assort Health
Top 10 Investor, CB Insights 5x Revenue Growth Stanford Founder
Reservation Deadline
Thu July 16 · 12:00pm ET
Full Deal Room →

Elevator Pitch: Assort Health builds AI agents for the healthcare "front door" — automating scheduling, referrals, intake, and follow-up directly inside providers' existing EHR systems. It has scaled to ~$27M in contracted ARR across 144 customers and 10,000 providers with 98% post-pilot retention, and is now raising a $100M+ Series C led by Menlo Ventures at a $1.2B valuation.

Founders Jon Wang, Co-CEO — Stanford M.S./B.S. in Medical Informatics, former MD candidate at UCSF, ex-Chief of Staff to the CEO at Elemy, and a prior YC-backed founder; Jeff Liu, Co-CEO — Duke Computer Science & Economics, previously led product engineering at Athelas. Advisors include Paul Ricci (founding CEO of Nuance) and Roy Rosin (Chief Innovation Officer, Penn Medicine).
AV's Access
Purple Arch Ventures and Spike Ventures led diligence and secured AV's co-investor allocation alongside new lead Menlo Ventures ($6.8B AUM), which is committing $65M — upsized from $40M, and more than half the round — through partners John Paul Sanday and Matt Murphy. Existing investors Lightspeed (which led the Series B) and Felicis are returning on a super pro rata basis with $8M and $20M, respectively.
Why Invest in Assort Health
  • Proven, at-scale traction, not a promise: Contracted ARR has grown from ~$2M to ~$27M in 15 months (8x revenue growth since Q4 2024), with ~$9M already implemented, a 97%+ pilot-to-paid conversion rate, and 98% logo retention. Management is targeting ~$60M CARR by year-end 2026 and ~$140M by year-end 2027.
  • A workflow-intelligence data moat that compounds: 125M+ patient interactions across 22 specialties and 84+ EHR integrations create a proprietary dataset that grows more valuable — and harder to replicate — with every deployment, turning patient access into a data business as much as a software one.
  • A high-conviction healthcare-AI syndicate: Menlo — a healthcare-AI specialist with a published "State of AI in Healthcare" thesis — is anchoring at $65M, while the investors closest to the business (Lightspeed and Felicis) increase exposure super pro rata. Assort was named to CB Insights' 2026 AI 100 and Forbes' Next Billion-Dollar Startups 2025.
Key Issues
  • High burn creates execution dependency: The company burns $4M–$6M per month, with OpEx scaling from ~$1.3M in 2025 to $18M+ in 2026. The path to $140M ARR requires efficient conversion of contracted ARR into implemented ARR — any slippage makes the entry price harder to justify.
  • Mid-market strength, enterprise question: The strongest proof points are mid-market practices with short procurement cycles. Cracking large health systems — the bulk of administrative spend — is materially slower and not yet proven.
  • A rich entry price in a converging market: $1.2B post-money is a mid-40s multiple on current CARR, and EHR incumbents like Epic and athenahealth, plus well-funded rivals like Qventus, are targeting the same workflows.

Lightfield
200%+ NRR 5x Revenue Growth Ex-Facebook
Reservation Deadline
Thu July 16 · 12:00pm ET
Full Deal Room →

Elevator Pitch: Lightfield is building the AI-native CRM — a living system of record that records calls, reads email, and updates accounts automatically, then runs the go-to-market work teams currently spread across 8+ tools. Built by the team behind Tome (25M+ users) around the two lessons that stalled it, ARR has grown ~15x in six months to ~$1.5M at 251% net dollar retention, and Tome's own backers followed them into the new company.

Founders Keith Peiris, CEO — co-founder/CEO of Tome (25M+ users, ~$81M raised); earlier grew Instagram Direct 10x to 500M MAU and led product at Facebook, credited on 25+ patents. Technical co-founders come from Facebook AI Research (Cornell PhD), the Pinterest Developer Platform, and Stanford search & retrieval; President Henri Liriani co-founded Tome and ran Messenger's billion-user core. GTM leaders are pulled from Gong, MuleSoft, and Salesforce.
AV's Access
Purple Arch Ventures led diligence and secured AV's allocation alongside new lead Maverick Ventures, which is writing $15M of the up-to-$30M round and taking a board seat for Ryan Isono — who led n8n at Series A, since valued at $5.2B. Tome's largest backers, Lightspeed (board seat, Lisa Han) and Greylock, are continuing behind the new thesis rather than starting from zero.
Why Invest in Lightfield
  • Built on the lessons of Tome, backed by Tome's investors: Tome raised $80M+ and scaled past 25M users, but stalled on the two things that mattered most — customer context and repeat usage. Lightfield is built directly around those failures, and Lightspeed and Greylock, who watched the first company up close, followed into the recap, with Lightspeed taking a board seat.
  • Exceptional early traction and efficiency: ARR climbed from ~$95K in November 2025 to ~$1.5M in May 2026 (~75% month-over-month) at a reported 251% net dollar retention, with 70%+ of signups organic, CAC payback compressed from 28 months to under 8, and 60 HubSpot and 15 Salesforce migrations already won.
  • A wedge into the stickiest seat in software: The CRM system of record turns over for fewer than 1% of businesses a year. Winning AI-native companies' first CRM is the same greenfield path HubSpot used to build a category, and captured customer context deepens the source of truth — raising switching costs with every interaction.
Key Issues
  • Adoption is broad but still shallow: Many of the "1,100+ active companies" are single-seat, and some show little recent activity, so the headline reads as top-of-funnel breadth rather than scaled penetration. The base skews to young, budget-constrained tech startups.
  • A conviction price in a crowded field: The $140M post-money is ~93x current ARR and ~13x a forward number the company still has to earn, against a growing set of AI-CRM rivals — Rox, Clarify, Day.ai, Reevo, Attio, and Monaco.
  • Proprietary technology vs. model reliance: The product only became viable once frontier models were good enough. How much of Lightfield is defensible IP versus orchestration on models it does not own is unproven — and pricing or capability shifts from a model provider could flow straight through.

A Note on Portfolio Strategy

Smart venture portfolios are built deliberately over years, with enough scale and diversification to give the asset class room to work.

Building a venture allocation solely through single-deal investments often requires significantly more capital and time, vs. a planned mix of funds and syndications.

Many investors have a Venture Plan with AV (see the "Planning" tab above), and dedicate approximately 50%–75% of their annual venture allocation to one or more AV funds, then supplement with selected syndication offerings. Submitted plans are not legally binding. Investors who submit plans unlock more personalized service.
